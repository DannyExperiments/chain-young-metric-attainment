## Messick's question and the candidate's scope

Scott Bryan Messick, *Continuous Automata, Compactness, and Young Measures*, PhD dissertation, Cornell University, May 2016, DOI 10.7298/X4D798BM. The conjecture is §3.3, p. 35, footnote 2, at PDF p. 46. After allowing that irrational distances in the alphabet could cause nonattainment, Messick conjectures attainment under the complementary rational-distance hypothesis. The footnote concerns the infimum in Definition 3.7. The ordinary Young metric of Definition 3.2 and the area Young metric of §3.2 are distinct definitions. Theorem 3.8 gives equivalence on rational step words, a comparison of metrics that does not give a minimizing edit chain. [Cornell record](https://doi.org/10.7298/X4D798BM) · [Definition and footnote](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e61833ea-c15d-40a3-8e93-e0310d91055d/content#page=46) · [Catalog Q114](https://github.com/10m-u/open-problems-catalog/blob/ca8d4d44ba3db527f19f28928c240168f545ecfe/problems/mathematics/logic-foundations-set-theory-part-1.md#q114).

- The thesis explicitly adopts almost-everywhere equivalence for words, including rational step words, at printed p. 30, PDF p. 41. This removes the endpoint-value ambiguity from the brackets used in the move displays. A common rational grid represents the equivalence classes without any pointwise boundary compatibility requirement. [Endpoint convention](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e61833ea-c15d-40a3-8e93-e0310d91055d/content#page=41).
- Definition 3.6, printed p. 34, takes words constant on intervals of a rational partition, including a rational total length. Definition 3.7, printed pp. 34–35, fixes the same domain `[0,ℓ]` along a finite chain. It expressly uses rational intervals. A vertical push on a constant interval of length `a` costs `a d_Σ(σ,τ)`. A neighbor swap of two adjacent constant intervals each of length `a` costs `a²`. These are the two operations enumerated in the definition. [Definitions](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e61833ea-c15d-40a3-8e93-e0310d91055d/content#page=45).
- Observations 3.10–3.11, printed p. 37, already use the common divisor of a chain's endpoints and decompose both operations on that grid. The thesis then develops a pseudo-word balancing argument for metric equivalence. Its grid reduction is already known and should be credited. Remark 3.23, printed p. 47, separately discusses an extended chain with additional length-changing operations. The conjecture at Definition 3.7 uses the fixed-length model. [Grid observations](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e61833ea-c15d-40a3-8e93-e0310d91055d/content#page=48) · [Length extension](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/e61833ea-c15d-40a3-8e93-e0310d91055d/content#page=58).

The compact-alphabet setting is Messick's ambient context. The recovered four-page Q114 manuscript states the sufficient condition using only the cross-distances `d(A_i,B_j)` between a letter in the source word and a letter in the target word. This condition is weaker than pairwise rational distances across the union of the letters, the headline in the readable intake. It implies the conjectured case of rational alphabet distances. The manuscript's two-letter example addresses Messick's suggestion of possible irrational-distance nonattainment within the rational-move model. Use the cross-distance condition if the mathematical review clears the recovered version. Avoid extending this scope to changing-length chains or arbitrary real breakpoints.

## Transport and optimization prior art

### Quadratic-form transport

Ruodu Wang and Zhenyuan Zhang, “Quadratic-form optimal transport,” *Mathematical Programming*, published 22 September 2025, DOI 10.1007/s10107-025-02282-5. Their general objective integrates a kernel against two independent draws from one coupling. The finite-support quadratic program is Appendix A. The Kendall sign-product kernel is Example 3.4 in the authors' September 2025 copy and Example 5 in arXiv v5 (4 June 2026). [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-025-02282-5) · [September 2025 author PDF](https://sas.uwaterloo.ca/~wang/papers/2025Wang-Zhang-MP.pdf) · [arXiv v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04658v5).

The Q114 objective is exactly an instance of this general framework, by direct comparison. If `X` is the finite run coupling of total mass `ℓ>0` and `π=X/ℓ`, take two independent draws `(i,j),(k,l)~π`. Write `d_ij=d(A_i,B_j)`. The kernel
```
(ℓ/2)(d_ij + d_kl) + (ℓ²/2) · 1[(i-k)(j-l)<0]
```
has expected value equal to the linear relabeling cost plus the Q114 crossing sum. The factor of two compensates for the two orientations of each strict crossing. This is the discordance probability component of Kendall's tau. With discrete run marginals, ties matter, so the crossing sum should not be identified with a fixed affine function of tau alone. The QOT paper supplies the optimization framework and discusses Kendall kernels, without identifying Messick's edit metric, proving the rational-grid lift, or treating his conjecture. The authors' 2026 follow-up on diamond transports focuses on other cost families. [Discrete QOT formulation](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.04658v5#A1) · [2026 follow-up](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22589).

### Rational minimizers and related edit metrics

- S. A. Vavasis, “Quadratic programming is in NP,” *Information Processing Letters* 36(2), 73–77 (1990), DOI 10.1016/0020-0190(90)90100-C. A. Del Pia, S. S. Dey and M. Molinaro summarize the rational optimal-solution consequence and the rational linear system that gives it in Theorem 3 of their preprint *Mixed-integer Quadratic Programming is in NP*. D. O. Plutenko's 2026 preprint treats attainment more generally over linearly ordered fields in Theorem 4.1. The rational quadratic minimizer step in the Q114 manuscript is an application of established theory. [Vavasis publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0020-0190(90)90100-C) · [Del Pia–Dey–Molinaro, Thm. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.4798#page=3) · [Plutenko](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17969v1).
- V. A. Cicirello, “Kendall tau sequence distance: Extending Kendall tau from ranks to sequences,” *EAI Endorsed Transactions on Industrial Networks and Intelligent Systems* 7(23), e1 (2020), DOI 10.4108/eai.13-7-2018.163925, describes the fixed finite-string minimum-adjacent-swap problem with equal symbol multiplicities. It supplies a nearby model for the inversion bookkeeping. Messick allows weighted letter substitutions and rational subdivisions of the time intervals, which change the optimization problem. ## Bounded verdict

No readable source in this search gives the exact fixed-length chain Young metric as the stated run-transport minimum, proves finite-chain attainment under rational cross-distances, or gives the recovered irrational-distance counterexample. The distinction rests on the metric identification and constructive lift from a rational plan to a legal finite chain. The fixed-grid decomposition, pair-inversion accounting, quadratic-form transport and rational quadratic attainment have prior literature and merit explicit attribution. Searches used both Messick titles and DOI citation trails, the exact metric and step-word terminology, and related discordance-kernel and adjacent-swap formulations. The LFCS gap and limited citation indexing rule out a definitive priority statement. This is a literature and source-fidelity assessment, with proof adjudication recorded separately.

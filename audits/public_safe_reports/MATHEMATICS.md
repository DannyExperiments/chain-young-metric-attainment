# Mathematical review notes

The same-width grid restriction is checked against Messick's Observations 3.10–3.11. A finite chain assigns labeled cell tokens to endpoint runs. Each token's push costs bound its direct relabeling cost by the triangle inequality. Every required reversal of two distinct tokens costs at least one adjacent exchange. The mandatory inversions for run pairs are disjoint.

For the reverse direction, impose increasing destination order within every source run and increasing source order within every target run. The resulting permutation has exactly the displayed pairwise inversions. Direct pushes followed by adjacent exchanges give a legal chain on a rational grid. Virtual swaps between equal letters preserve the word and can be omitted.

The polytope is compact. The stationarity equations of the rational quadratic on the minimal face of a minimizer are rational linear equations. A nearby rational solution stays feasible and gives the same objective. In the two-run irrational example the minimizer is unique, and every finite rational-grid chain induces rational transport entries. Exact finite replays are recorded separately.
